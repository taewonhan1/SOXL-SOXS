# Momentum Survives, Flipping to SOXS Doesn't

Nothing published online shows a rule for switching between SOXL and SOXS, or between TQQQ/SQQQ, SPXL/SPXS or any other bull/bear leveraged pair, that has made money after costs on data it was not fitted to. The rule-complete switchers are mostly daily close-to-close systems, intraday SOXL claims come from vendors and authors (Tickeron's 551% a year at a 79% win rate) with no costs or out-of-sample test, and the only intraday SOXL/SOXS system found with a third-party-tracked record finished at **−2.1% with a profit factor of 1.0** and stopped trading. The research underneath is real but thin: the morning's move does predict the close (Gao et al. 2018; Baltussen et al. 2021), and the Concretum opening-range-breakout and "noise area" papers built TQQQ and SPY systems claiming 1,484% and 1,985% on that idea, but independent replications find that about 2.2¢ a share of slippage erases the QQQ breakout, that it nets roughly zero on five indices over 2015–2026, and that the noise-area strategy lost money in 2025 and 2026. This repository's pre-registered tests land in the same place: going with SOXL's move from the open beats a random side by **0.2–0.3% a trade** in 2011–2018, 2019–2025 and 2026 (p ≈ 0.002), mostly through the SOXL side, but the profit after costs is not established (reality-check p ≈ 0.2), the 15-minute breakout failed both untouched periods, and every stop-and-reverse design lost. The closest test of flipping on 1-minute momentum, an EMA crossover that switched between SOXL and SOXS about 16 times a day, captured **about 2 bps gross per flip** and lost after costs in every year from 2019 to 2026. The practical frictions point the same way: decay barely matters within a session but SOXS decays about twice as fast as SOXL overnight, SOXS's ten reverse splits swing its tick cost between about 2 and 50 bps, each switch pays 2–3 bps of spread, the pattern-day-trader rule stopped binding most margin accounts on June 4, 2026, and wash sales catch same-fund re-entries rather than SOXL↔SOXS flips. For a trader who switches sides whenever the 1-minute trend turns, the evidence supports a narrower habit: act on large moves (3% or more) established by 10:00–11:00, mostly on the SOXL side, and go flat rather than reverse into SOXS on small dips or crosses of the open.

## Published SOXL–SOXS switchers are daily, unaudited and long-biased

Most rule-complete SOXL↔SOXS switchers decide once a day, at or near the close, and none has an independent audit. Their thresholds read like fitted parameters: "SOXL Growth v2.4.5 RL" switches on cut-offs such as RSI(32) ≤ 62.1995, and a third-party Python port of it trades at the close with no commission or slippage terms ([IznogoudQc/soxl-growth](https://github.com/IznogoudQc/soxl-growth)). SiliconCycle, an open-source daily SOXL/SOXS/cash system tuned by an automated loop, reports a **CAGR of 8.9–12.6% with drawdowns of −75% to −77%** over 2010–2026; in March 2026 it stood 51% below its equity high and had not fired a SOXS signal since May 2025 ([axesprayor/siliconcycle-strategy](https://github.com/axesprayor/siliconcycle-strategy)). Composer, TradingView, Seeking Alpha and Tickeron pages could be read only through search-engine summaries, and Reddit not at all, so rules posted only in those communities are missing. The two records not written by the systems' sellers point the other way. Collective2's tracked intraday system made 176 trades in 2021 at a 47.2% win rate and a profit factor of 1.0 before going dormant, and a public forward paper log of a SOXL/SOXS/TQQQ/SQQQ momentum system recorded **39 trades from April to September 2026 at a 46.2% win rate and a loss**, despite catching a +137.9% SOXL run and without ever trading SOXS or SQQQ ([Collective2](https://collective2.com/details/134901681); [mrpink970/4-etf-trading-plan](https://github.com/mrpink970/4-etf-trading-plan)). The Korean VWAP bot is the telling case for a switcher: its author added a SOXS "dual momentum" side on 2026-04-28, demoted it the same day, and on 2026-05-10 replaced it with "a single long (SOXL) momentum architecture" ([KIS bot](https://github.com/pipios4006-boop/KIS-API-Python-Trading-Bot-Example)). A Korean e-book of twelve "1-minute chart" SOXL/SOXS strategies advertises a "verified win rate of over 80%" ([kmong](https://kmong.com/gig/690378)), far above any win rate measured independently. Educators' rules of thumb are timing filters rather than flip rules: let the open run ("it's almost always best to let it run till at least 10 o'clock"), take direction from the day's VWAP against the prior day's, stop opening trades late and go flat before the close, and treat SOXS as a short-hold tool ([Brian Cellars](https://briancellars.substack.com/p/getting-ready-for-the-next-market/comments); [KIS bot](https://github.com/pipios4006-boop/KIS-API-Python-Trading-Bot-Example)). None of these comes with a test that includes costs.

The widely followed Korean SOXL methods are not switching systems at all. Laoer's 무한매수법 ("infinite buying") splits capital into 20 or 40 parts, buys daily with limit-on-close orders, and for SOXL sells a quarter of the position at a "star point" of average cost × (1 + (20 − 2T)%), where T is the number of parts bought so far ([pbdfinance](https://www.pbdfinance.com/2024/09/v22-v30.html)). The 떨사오팔 ("buy the dip, sell the rip") grid bot reports a 49.3% CAGR against 37.7% for SOXL buy-and-hold over 2010–2026, assuming fills at the close with no slippage, and hit its −33.9% marked-to-market drawdown on 2026-07-29 ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iSEIypwB2o0)). Switching into SOXS happens as a crowd habit, and in 2026 the SOXS leg lost. Korean investors net-bought **$1.16B of SOXL while it fell 28.8%** in late March and sold $903M into the 28.7% rebound; SOXS then became their top net buy for April 16–22, during an 18-session semiconductor rally in which SOXS fell 35.4% over April 16–24 alone and 62.7% from its February entry window ([Money Today, Apr 6](https://www.mt.co.kr/world/2026/04/06/2026040612250035085); [Money Today, Apr 27](https://www.mt.co.kr/world/2026/04/27/2026042712151283577)).

For the other paired funds, the best-documented systems do not flip into the bear fund at all. Gayed & Bilello's Dow Award paper rotates between leveraged stocks and T-bills and ignores switching costs ([CXO](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/volatility-effects/leveraging-the-u-s-stock-market-based-on-sma-rules/)), an open-source TQQQ bot's default engine "never holds SQQQ" ([WildWest000](https://github.com/WildWest000/tqqq-trading-strategy)), and Lumibot's bull/bear template codifies the one universal pair rule: never hold a fund and its inverse together, and when switching, sell the whole opposite side before buying, in the same session ([Lumibot](https://lumibot.lumiwealth.com/agents_example_bull_bear_leveraged_etf.html)). No published rules with results were found for SPXL/SPXS, UPRO/SPXU, TNA/TZA, LABU/LABD, FAS/FAZ, TECL/TECS or NUGT/DUST, and **no study compares reversing into the bear fund with simply going flat**. On buying the inverse fund versus shorting the bull fund, only forum material exists: SQQQ reportedly costs under 1% a year to borrow ([Elite Trader](https://www.elitetrader.com/et/threads/long-tqqq-vs-short-sqqq.352452/)), and bear funds keep reverse-splitting, as SQQQ did 1-for-5 in November 2024 and again in November 2025 ([Nasdaq Trader](https://nasdaqtrader.com/TraderNews.aspx?id=ECA2024-536); [MIAX](https://www.miaxglobal.com/sites/default/files/alert-files/SQQQ1_Reverse_Split_57665.pdf)).

## Intraday momentum is real but small, and it pays in crashes

The academic anchor is Gao, Han, Li & Zhou's "Market intraday momentum" (Journal of Financial Economics, 2018). In SPY from 1993 to 2013, the return from the prior close to 10:00 predicted the 15:30–16:00 return with an R² of 1.6% in sample and 1.4% out of sample, and trading the last half hour on that sign earned **6.67% a year with a Sharpe near 1.08**, against 0.29 for buy-and-hold, roughly 2.6 bps a day ([SSRN](https://www.ssrn.com/abstract=2552752); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/325364670_Market_Intraday_Momentum)). Predictability was stronger on volatile, high-volume, recession and macro-news days, and QQQ, the closest proxy for tech, had the weakest out-of-sample R² of the ETFs tested, 0.70% ([paper PDF](https://assets.super.so/e46b77e7-ee08-445e-b43f-4ffd88ae0a0e/files/ee7dac49-530b-4950-b5d0-e0b5eee08f2e.pdf)). The pattern generalized to 12 of 16 developed markets ([Li, Sakkas & Urquhart](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S138641812100001X)) and to more than 60 futures markets over 1974–2020, where Baltussen, Da, Lammers & Martens found the rest-of-day return predicts the last 30 minutes with asset-class Sharpe ratios of 0.87–1.73 and traced it to hedging by short-gamma option dealers and by leveraged ETFs ([SSRN 3760365](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3760365)).

Leveraged ETFs matter here because their rebalancing is mechanical and always runs with the day. Restoring L× exposure after an index move r requires trading L(L−1)·A·r, where A is the fund's assets: six times SOXL's assets and twelve times SOXS's, both in the index's direction, so the bull and bear funds reinforce rather than offset each other ([Cheng & Madhavan](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1539120)). At July 2026 net assets of $19.44B and $1.61B ([SOXL N-PORT](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1424958/000119312526404435/primary_doc.xml); [SOXS N-PORT](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1424958/000119312526404519/primary_doc.xml)), that comes to roughly **$1.4B of semiconductor exposure per 1% index move and about $4.1B on a 3% day**, more than the $1.04B per 1% that Tuzun estimated for the entire US leveraged-ETF complex in 2012 ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/pubs/feds/2013/201348/201348abs.html)), in a year when market-wide leveraged-ETF rebalancing reportedly hit a record $50B a day ([Benzinga](https://www.benzinga.com/etfs/broad-u-s-equity-etfs/26/07/60264868/leveraged-etf-rebalancing-hits-50-billion-as-traders-warn-of-a-powerful-force-now-driving-us-market-volatility)). Barbon, Beckmeyer, Buraschi & Moerke measured that a one-standard-deviation rise in leveraged-ETF rebalancing flow lifts last-30-minute returns by 430% of their mean, reversing at the next open ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/p/chf/rpseri/rp2240.html)), but also found the effect "decreasing significantly over time" because perfectly predictable flows attract liquidity providers ([SSRN 3925725](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3925725)), and Ivanov & Lenkey showed that fund flows cut rebalancing demand by up to 85% ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1386418117302604)). Predictable flows also invite front-running: a 2:45 pm rule anticipating leveraged-ETF rebalancing made 0.60% gross a trade over 2006–2011 ([CXO on Shum et al.](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/volatility-effects/front-running-leveraged-etfs-at-the-end-of-the-day/)), and a 2026 paper on Korean single-stock leveraged ETFs models arbitrageurs who buy ahead of the closing rebalance and then sell into the demand they helped create ([Zhao](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03703)).

Out of sample, the US evidence has weakened. Rosa (2022) found that predictability in the overnight-return version "disappears in the out-of-sample period" and survives only as a regime-dependent effect, which a rule that trades only on strong signals captures better than one that is always in ([Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/fut.22375)). A 2024 Bayesian study of the last 30 minutes found intraday momentum has "diminished over time" ([MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2227-9091/12/11/180)). A vendor's 2026 test on 1,085 S&P 500 sessions found a flat slope (+0.006, t 0.6) except on the 15% of closes when 0DTE option dealers were short gamma ([FirmTape](https://dev.to/firmtape/intraday-momentum-is-dead-in-the-0dte-era-we-measured-it-on-1085-spx-sessions-43g0)), which fits research showing that dealers' net gamma is usually positive in the 0DTE era, a condition that favors reversal over momentum ([Dim, Eraker & Vilkov](https://ssrn.com/abstract=4692190)).

The practitioner papers built tradable systems on this foundation. All three trade QQQ or SPY long and short and use TQQQ only to get leverage; none tests a bear fund on its own. Zarattini & Aziz's **opening-range breakout** (2023) trades QQQ at 09:35 in the direction of the first 5-minute candle (no trade on a doji), stops at that candle's opposite extreme, exits at 10R or the close, and risks 1% of equity per trade, capped at the 4× day-trading leverage limit; it claims **1,484% for TQQQ over 2016–February 2023** against 169% for QQQ buy-and-hold, assuming $0.0005 a share in commission and no slippage ([SSRN 4416622](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4416622); [replication](https://github.com/giovannibrusco/zarattini-2023-orb-qqq)). Their **"noise area"** paper with Barbon (2024) sets a band around the open equal to the 14-day average absolute move from the open at each minute, widened by overnight gaps. It checks only on the hour and half hour from 10:00, goes long above the band and short below it, trails a stop at the band or VWAP, is flat at 16:00 and sizes positions to 2% daily volatility, and it reports **1,985% on SPY over 2007–2024 with a Sharpe of 1.33** ([SSRN 4824172](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4824172); [replication spec](https://github.com/giovannibrusco/zarattini-2024-momentum-spy/blob/main/docs/SPEC.md)). Their **VWAP system** is always in the market, long above session VWAP and short below, and claims $25,000 grew to $2.09M in TQQQ from 2018 to September 2023 after **$400,616 in commissions** ([Concretum](https://concretumgroup.com/volume-weighted-average-price-vwap-the-holy-grail-for-day-trading-systems/); [Bear Bull Traders](https://members.bearbulltraders.com/magic-of-vwap-the-holy-grail-of-day-trading-systems/)). By the replicators' reading, the noise-area profits rise with volatility (a Sharpe near 3.5 when VIX exceeds 40) and lean on 2008, while the breakout's profits concentrate in 2020–2022 ([replication spec](https://github.com/giovannibrusco/zarattini-2024-momentum-spy/blob/main/docs/SPEC.md); [ORB replication](https://github.com/giovannibrusco/zarattini-2023-orb-qqq)).

## Independent re-tests shrink headline returns to roughly zero

An independent replication of the QQQ breakout reproduces the paper (1,775 trades, Sharpe 1.06 against 1.12) and then shows how thin the edge is. The gross edge is about 7¢ a share, about 75% of trades exit at the stop and only 2–3% reach the 10R target, and adding 2¢ of entry slippage and 4¢ of stop slippage cuts the Sharpe to **0.23** (CAGR 2.7%, maximum drawdown 43.9%). Break-even comes at **about 2.2¢ a share** of slippage, against QQQ's spread of about 1¢, and 2022 alone produced 76% of the best filtered variant's profit and 38% of the plain rule's ([giovannibrusco](https://github.com/giovannibrusco/zarattini-2023-orb-qqq)). A September 2026 re-test on CFDs tracking the Nasdaq-100, S&P 500, Dow, DAX and FTSE over 2015–2026, read only in summary, found the rule still beats a random direction gross (+0.13R on the Nasdaq-100) but nets nothing distinguishable from zero in any market, with four of five negative ([mql5](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/776235)). A 2026 study of 14 families of intraday price-and-volume signals on micro Nasdaq futures found that none survived walk-forward testing plus a 2-point round-trip cost ([Mesfin](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6709401)). The "stocks in play" variant's Sharpe of 2.81 owes almost everything to picking the day's 20 most abnormally active stocks, since a plain breakout across the market made 0.48 in the paper's own data ([Concretum](https://concretumgroup.com/a-profitable-day-trading-strategy-for-the-u-s-equity-market/)); one replication found the assumed order in which a bar hit its high and low alone worth 6.39 Sharpe points, and its live paper-trading stops slipped 3–5R on gap-throughs ([hsaeed22058](https://github.com/hsaeed22058/strategy-backtest)).

The noise-area strategy is the best replicated of the three, and the clearest case of decay after publication. A replication on independent data matched the in-sample Sharpe of 1.34 and then measured **0.39 out of sample (May 2024–March 2026)**, a statistically significant decline (p < 0.001) ([PazSheimy](https://github.com/PazSheimy/spy-intraday-momentum-oos)). Another made +25.8% in 2022 while SPY fell 19.5%, then **−4.9% in 2025 and −12.9% in 2026 through July**, and its E-mini S&P 500 futures version had a Sharpe of −0.07; with costs near 0.4 bps per round trip against an expectancy of −0.6 bps a trade, the signal failed, not the costs ([validation](https://github.com/codecat-ops/zarattini-2024-momentum-spy/blob/main/reports/validation.md); [futures validation](https://github.com/codecat-ops/zarattini-2024-momentum-spy/blob/main/reports/validation_es.md)). A pre-registered grid of 27 variants found the paper's own configuration best in sample (Deflated Sharpe 0.972) and still only 0.16 out of sample, so Maróy's optimized exits, claimed to reach a Sharpe above 3, did not help ([grid experiment](https://github.com/codecat-ops/zarattini-2024-momentum-spy/blob/main/reports/maroy_experiment.md); [SSRN 5095349](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5095349)). The replicator judges the edge "compressed" but not provably dead, since the paper itself shows a 2012–2015 lull followed by recovery ([conclusions](https://github.com/codecat-ops/zarattini-2024-momentum-spy/blob/main/docs/CONCLUSIONS.md)). No independent test of the always-in VWAP system was found.

Daily rules replicate as drawdown reducers rather than return enhancers. A pre-registered 2026 test of a 200-day trend filter in the Gayed & Bilello style, run on 18 ETFs outside its development sample, cut maximum drawdown in all 18 but lowered returns in 13, and the author's verdict was "no arm passes" ([evotrader](https://github.com/RafaelPF07/evotrader/pull/4)). A RealTest replication on TQQQ reportedly cut the drawdown from −81.6% to −57.2% but ended with about $1.12M against $3.96M for buy-and-hold, because a slow filter sells after the crash and buys back after the rebound ([setup4alpha](https://setup4alpha.substack.com/p/tested-award-winning-trading-strategy-realtest)). Composer's "out-of-sample" figures for the TQQQ For The Long Term forks (49–81% a year) are platform simulations since each symphony was created, Composer reportedly keeps that start date even when a symphony's rules are later edited, and no independent replication exists ([Composer FTLT](https://www.composer.trade/trading-strategies/tqqq-ftlt-with-a-bit-of-soxl-and--V8PMHSkT4yH87TLz2B7K); [Composer](https://www.composer.trade/whats-new)).

These outcomes match the base rates. Across 97 published predictors, returns fall 58% after publication ([McLean & Pontiff](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/jofi.12365)); across 215 commercial strategies, the median Sharpe falls from 1.20 in backtest to 0.31 live ([Suhonen, Lennkh & Perez](https://www.pm-research.com/content/iijpormgmt/43/2/90)); and among 888 Quantopian algorithms, backtest Sharpe explained less than 2.5% of the variation in out-of-sample Sharpe ([Wiecki et al.](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/All-That-Glitters-Is-Not-Gold:-Comparing-Backtest-a-Wiecki-Campbell/b028c2d0b2145e85d543fb21ad3fcf896c932ab5)). Fewer than 1% of Taiwanese day traders over 1992–2006 outperformed consistently ([Barber, Lee, Liu & Odean](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/The%20Cross-Section%20of%20Speculator%20Skill.pdf)), and 97% of Brazilian index-futures day traders who kept at it for more than 300 days lost money ([Chague, De-Losso & Giovannetti](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3423101)). The published designs also differ on the one choice that matters most for a switcher. The breakout takes one trade a day and stays flat after a stop; the noise-area model is flat by default and takes the opposite side only if price later breaks the opposite band at a half-hour check; the VWAP model reverses on every cross. The always-in design is the one with the largest commission bill and no surviving independent test.

## This repository's tests land where the replications did

This repository's studies are the independent SOXL/SOXS replication the published material lacks. The rules were fixed in a registry before any run, then tested on a 2011–2018 history no rule had seen and on a sealed 2026 holdout opened once, and judged against two benchmarks: a random choice of side, and White's reality check, which resamples the whole search to ask whether its best results beat zero by chance ([RESULTS.md](../analysis/strategies/RESULTS.md)). None of the published SOXL/SOXS rules faced any of these tests. Side by side, the results line up row for row.

The core finding reproduces the academic shape: real, small, and mostly eaten by costs. Going with SOXL's move from its 09:30 open (SOXL on up moves, SOXS on down moves) at checks from 10:00 to 14:00 and holding to 15:55 beats a random side by **+29, +19 and +21 bps a trade** in 2011–2018, 2019–2025 and 2026, and a random side matches that only about 0.2% of the time ([era split](../analysis/strategies/intraday_trend_odds/luck_check_by_era.csv); [luck check](../analysis/strategies/intraday_trend_odds/luck_check.csv)), the same "beats a random direction gross" pattern the five-index breakout replication found. After costs the average across the 35 cells is +6.8 bps, which the reality check produces by luck about one time in five ([luck check](../analysis/strategies/intraday_trend_odds/luck_check.csv)). Before 2026, costs rather than the signal did most of the damage: breakout round trips cost 21 bps in 2011–2018 and 9 bps in 2019–2025, while in 2026 a random side roughly broke even (+5.3 bps a trade). If continuation holds at its 15-year level, today's costs would leave about +0.15–0.25% a trade, which the repository labels a forecast, not a result ([RESULTS.md](../analysis/strategies/RESULTS.md)). That leaves SOXL where the futures replication left the noise-area strategy: whether the signal persists is now the binding question. Vendor win rates of 79–84% also sit far above anything measured here: no rule in this repository won more than about 65% of its trades, and the profitable ones won through the size of trend-day winners rather than their frequency ([RESULTS.md](../analysis/strategies/RESULTS.md)).

Side and size follow the literature's regime story. Pooled across eras, buying SOXL on up moves is established against a random side (p ≈ 0.005) and buying SOXS on down moves is not (p ≈ 0.07), yet in 2019–2025, the era containing the 2020 crash and the 2022 bear market, SOXS on down moves (+8.4 bps) beat SOXL on up moves (+5.7 bps) ([by side](../analysis/strategies/intraday_trend_odds/luck_check_by_side.csv)). The side that pays tracks the prevailing regime, as the crash-concentrated profits of the published breakout and noise-area systems would predict. Size works like Rosa's signal-strength threshold: a SOXL drop of **3% or more by 10:00–11:00** made SOXS positive in every era (+0.11%, +0.27% and +0.34% a trade), while drops of 1–3% tended to bounce and lost (−0.10%, −0.12% and −1.19%) ([side × size × time](../analysis/strategies/intraday_trend_odds/luck_check_side_size_time.csv)). Fading lost about 0.5% a trade in every era, and chasing moves of 6% or more at 10:00 averaged nothing even though 69% of those days ended as trend days, because most of the move had already happened ([RESULTS.md](../analysis/strategies/RESULTS.md)). The educators' "let it run till at least 10 o'clock" fits this pattern better than any minute-level trigger does.

The opening-range breakout followed the published breakout's arc from promise to nothing. The 15-minute version was a near-miss on 2019–2025, with +48.9 bps a trade in validation (t 1.74) and every validation quarter positive. It then lost 12 bps a trade on 2011–2018 with **no positive year out of eight**, and 1.9 bps a trade in the sealed 2026 holdout even though 31% of 2026 sessions were trend days ([RESULTS.md](../analysis/strategies/RESULTS.md); [holdout summary](../analysis/strategies/holdout_2026/summary.csv)). The causes mirror the QQQ replication's dependence on 2022: in 2011–2018 the gross edge was a third as large, costs were more than twice as high and trend days less than half as common, with SOXL's median daily range at 3.6–4.9% against 6–10% in 2020–2025 ([RESULTS.md](../analysis/strategies/RESULTS.md)). Noise-boundary momentum fared no better outside 2019–2025 (see the table). The forward-tracked version of the continuation idea is the simplest flat-by-default rule: at 11:00, if SOXL is 3% or more from its open, go with the move, stop at the open and exit at 15:55. It was positive after costs in all three eras (+12.9, +20.4 and +18.0 bps, t ≤ 1.2) ([midday grid](../analysis/strategies/midday_trend/grid.csv)).

Every stop-and-reverse design lost, and the closest analogue to flipping on 1-minute momentum lost most consistently. When SOXL first moved 2–4% one way and a 1-minute close then came back through its opening price, taking the opposite fund lost in **7 of 9** era-by-size cells; in 2026 the 2% version lost 0.99% a trade over about 103 trades a year, because a failed move usually turns into chop rather than a trend the other way ([flip test](../analysis/strategies/intraday_trend_odds/flip_at_open.csv)). A 1-minute EMA(9/21) crossover that held SOXL when bullish and SOXS when bearish, reversing on every cross, traded about **16 times a day** out of sample (October 2024–September 2026), captured **+1.9 bps gross per flip** and lost 13.5 bps net; its SOXL legs lost 3.4 bps and its SOXS legs, traded at SOXS's then-low share price, lost 23.6 bps ([battery](../analysis/backtests/output/battery_summary.csv)). A SOXL-only version that shorts SOXL on bearish crosses still lost 1.7 bps a trade in 2026 with commissions set to zero, so the spread alone exceeds the edge ([yearly battery](../analysis/backtests/output/battery_yearly.csv)). Following VWAP crosses, the published VWAP system's rule, made money gross only in 2020–2022 and has been flat to negative since, the same rise and fade the noise-area replications show ([yearly battery](../analysis/backtests/output/battery_yearly.csv)). The pre-registered 1-minute momentum scalps had no edge even before costs, and their bearish flag trades in SOXS lost 17–31 bps a trade; at 1–30 minutes SOXL behaves like a random walk, continuing the same way only 48.5–50.0% of the time after a 2σ minute ([RESULTS.md](../analysis/strategies/RESULTS.md); [continuation test](../analysis/behavior/output/dir_continuation_after_large_moves.csv)).

The close is where this repository departs from the classic literature and agrees with its newer critics. Instead of momentum into the close, SOXL's last 30 minutes lean against the day: on days when SOXX had moved 2–3% by 15:30 the last half hour continued the move only 32.9% of the time, and the closing auction prints a median 6.2 bps against the day's move ([rebalancing test](../analysis/behavior/output/close_letf_rebalancing_by_index_move.csv); [auction test](../analysis/behavior/output/close_last10min_and_auction.csv)). Trading last-30-minute momentum earned +30.6 bps gross a trade in 2020 but lost 31.2 bps a trade net out of sample ([yearly battery](../analysis/backtests/output/battery_yearly.csv); [battery](../analysis/backtests/output/battery_summary.csv)). That is what Barbon's decay and Zhao's pre-positioning predict once a $4B rebalance is fully anticipated, and it is a concrete reason the continuation tests exit at 15:55 rather than holding the day's winner into the auction. The repository's own limits are real: fills are modeled on 1-minute bars with SOXS exits mirrored from SOXL's chart (a median mapping error of about 5 bps), the 2026 down-move sample is only 224 signals, and the promising 3–4% cluster at 10:00–11:00 was found by looking at 70 cells ([RESULTS.md](../analysis/strategies/RESULTS.md)). Before August 25, 2021, SOXL also tracked the PHLX Semiconductor Index rather than today's NYSE Semiconductor Index, so the 2011–2018 test ran on a different benchmark ([SOXL prospectus](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1424958/000119312526078540/d50601d497k.htm)).

## Frictions reward fewer switches, the SOXL side and flat closes

Decay is a multi-day cost, not an intraday one. Both funds pursue ±3× only for a single trading day and reset at each 4:00 pm close ([SOXL prospectus](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1424958/000119312526078540/d50601d497k.htm)), so an open-to-close hold tracks closely: over 2021–2026, SOXL's open-to-close move ran a median 2.96× the index proxy's and SOXS's −3.01× ([repo computation](../research_notes/SOXL%20SOXS%20switching%20research/practical_issues.md)). Held overnight, a daily-reset fund falls behind L times the index's log return by about (L² − L)σ²/2 a day, where σ is the index's daily volatility: −3σ² for SOXL and **−6σ² for SOXS**. That formula reproduces Direxion's own table, in which a flat index at 50% volatility costs SOXL 52.8% and SOXS 77.7% over a year ([SOXS prospectus](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1424958/000119312526078538/d48414d497k.htm)), and at 2026's roughly 50% index volatility it amounts to about 0.3% a day for SOXL and 0.6% for SOXS. The calendar record shows the asymmetry. In 2022, when the index proxy fell 36%, SOXS gained only 15.5%; in 2024 the index rose 12.2% while SOXL fell 13.0% and SOXS fell 61.4%; and from the start of 2026 to September 25, SOXL rose 260% while SOXS lost 94.8% ([repo computation](../research_notes/SOXL%20SOXS%20switching%20research/practical_issues.md)). Fees and swap financing, which accrue only to overnight holders, add to that bill: SOXL trailed three times its index proxy by 7.0 bps a day on average in 2026, a shortfall that includes its fees, the financing on $44.9B of swaps and the proxy's omitted dividends ([repo computation](../research_notes/SOXL%20SOXS%20switching%20research/practical_issues.md); [SOXL N-PORT](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1424958/000119312526404435/primary_doc.xml)).

Within the day, the relevant effect is leverage drift. Exposure is fixed at the prior close, so switching into SOXS after a +5% index morning buys −3.71× per new dollar, while SOXL then carries only 2.74× ([repo computation](../research_notes/SOXL%20SOXS%20switching%20research/practical_issues.md)), the "greater than, or less than" effect the prospectus warns of under Intra-Day Investment Risk ([SOXL prospectus](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1424958/000119312526078540/d50601d497k.htm)). A single headline can also undo a flip within minutes: at 13:19 on April 9, 2025, SOXS fell 10.71% in one minute and 20.7% in five ([largest moves](../analysis/behavior/output/tails_top10_moves.csv)).

Costs depend on the share price, and reverse splits keep resetting SOXS's. SOXS has reverse-split ten times since 2011, a cumulative factor of 960,000,000 to 1, including 1-for-20 on March 5, 2026 and 1-for-10 on July 15, 2026, each announced about four to five weeks ahead ([Direxion 497, Feb 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1424958/000119312526037667/d80567d497.htm); [Direxion 497, Jun 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1424958/000119312526265931/d101574d497.htm)). Because SOXS usually quotes one cent wide, its cost in bps is one cent divided by its price, and the March split cut that from **51.8 bps to 2.5 bps** ([repo computation](../research_notes/SOXL%20SOXS%20switching%20research/practical_issues.md)), which is why the EMA switch's SOXS legs lost so much more than its SOXL legs. In the current regime a marketable switch costs about **2.1–3.4 bps** (effective spreads of 1.89 bps for SOXL and 2.32 bps for SOXS), about 5 bps in the first half hour, and SOXL's spread widened 4–5× on the June 2026 stress days ([quoted spreads](../analysis/microstructure/output/quoted_spread_by_bucket.csv); [effective spreads](../analysis/microstructure/output/effective_spread.csv); [stress days](../analysis/microstructure/output/stress_vs_normal.csv)). At 150 switches a year that is 3–5% of the capital traded; at the ~16 flips a day of a 1-minute crossover it is 34–54 bps a day, more than the roughly 30 bps a day such a system grosses.

The remaining frictions concern how and where the account trades, and several changed in 2026.

These frictions mostly reinforce the evidence above. The end of the PDT rule and the coming overnight sessions make high-turnover flipping easier without adding any edge, the wash-sale rule penalizes re-entering the same fund after a loss, which a frequent flipper does constantly, and shorting SOXL to avoid SOXS's costs runs into Rule 201 on about a quarter of 2026 sessions. Several points remain unverified: each broker's PDT adoption date, broker house margin on 3× funds, whether SOXL and SOXS could ever be ruled substantially identical, and the units of the swap-financing spreads in Direxion's N-PORT filings, which govern how much an overnight SOXL holder pays.

## Conclusion

Three bodies of evidence, the academic literature, its independent replications and this repository's pre-registered SOXL/SOXS tests, agree on one picture. Intraday direction in these funds is a real gross effect, worth about 0.2–0.3% a trade in SOXL, concentrated in large moves established by mid-morning and in volatile regimes, paid mostly on the side of the prevailing trend, and lost mainly to turnover. What every source penalizes is the reflex a 1-minute flipping method is built on: reversing into the opposite fund when a short-horizon trend turns. Stop-and-reverse on VWAP or EMA crosses, flipping at the open and fading all lost, while the designs that held up longest (Gao's single half-hour bet, the noise-area model's flat-by-default band, this repository's 11:00 check) act once, on a large move, and let a failed move end in cash rather than in SOXS. Turned into a rule set to test forward, that means defaulting to flat, taking SOXL on moves of 3% or more at 10:00–11:00 checks and SOXS only on drops of 3% or more, stopping at the open, exiting by 15:55, and never treating a cross of the open or a 1-minute turn as a reason to switch sides.

What would change this view is forward evidence, and 2026 is an unusually informative test. Costs have fallen so far, with SOXL's multi-tick spread and SOXS at a post-split price, that a random side broke even, so the continuation should now show up as profit, or the post-2019 edge was a feature of that regime. The published record warns which way to lean: the noise-area edge faded within two years of publication, and the breakout's profits had already bunched into 2020–2022 before it was published. The repository's forward log, which judges each rule after 60 trades, is a stricter test than any published SOXL/SOXS claim has faced, and it is the only route by which a flipping rule could earn trust.
