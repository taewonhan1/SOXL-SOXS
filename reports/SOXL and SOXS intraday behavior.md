# SOXL and SOXS Deliver Movement, Not Direction

SOXL and SOXS are close to ideal vehicles for high-turnover intraday trading in every mechanical respect, and they offer almost nothing in predictable direction at scalping horizons. Measured on 2022–2026 Massive (formerly Polygon.io) data with the scripts in this repository, they deliver a median daily range near **7%** (about 3× SOXX and 5× QQQ), 1-minute volatility of **29–31 bps**, prints in essentially every minute from 04:00 to 20:00 with no detected halts, and regular-hours effective spreads near **2 bps**, which puts the quoted spread at about **a tenth of a typical 1-minute move**, the same ratio as NVDA and SPY. Yet SOXL's minute-level path is a random walk scaled up threefold: autocorrelations stay within ±0.03, variance ratios within 6% of one, large moves continue 48.5–50.3% of the time, and opening-range breaks, VWAP excursions and day shapes match their random benchmarks, while the few significant tendencies are worth a few bps, appear equally in unlevered SOXX and QQQ, and changed sign between regimes. The durable structure is mechanical: time-of-day profiles in which SOXL's quoted spread falls from 8.5 to 1.5 bps across the session, an intraday beta that follows the leverage-drift formula L(1+r)/(1+L·r) almost exactly, and a tight SOXL–SOXS mirror, whereas SOXS's apparent mean reversion and its cost in bps are artifacts of where its price sits in the reverse-split cycle. The data is sufficient for minute-level tracking and backtesting: bars down to one second and full trade and NBBO history from the 2010 listing, essentially gap-free and reproducible minute bars since 2022, and a repository toolkit, `soxlab`, with 97 look-ahead-tested features, a price-aware cost model, a conservative harness and a daily tracker, all passing 32 tests. What is missing is options and futures history before late 2024, any intraday fair value (no ICE index, no iNAV), holdings, flows and depth of book; and a first 16-probe backtest battery found one fragile survivor, a 15-minute opening-range breakout on SOXL that netted **+24.1 bps per trade out of sample** with a t-statistic of 1.19, no significance after multiple-testing adjustment and losses in 2026 to date.

## A 7% daily range, concentrated at the open, overnight and in jumps

Two windows anchor the price measurements: 2022-01-03 to 2024-09-30 ("2022–24", 685 full sessions) and 2024-10-01 to 2026-09-25 ("2024–26", 493 full sessions), computed from Massive minute, second and daily bars for SOXL, SOXS and six comparators, with half-days excluded and regular-hours (RTH, 09:30–16:00 ET) minute coverage at essentially 100% ([data_coverage.csv](../analysis/behavior/output/data_coverage.csv)). The two funds carry an identical and very large volatility budget. In 2024–26 SOXL's median daily high–low range was **6.68% of the open** and SOXS's **6.98%**; ranges exceeded 5% on 75–77% of days and 10% on about 23%, and the median 14-day average true range (ATR) ran near 9% ([vol_daily_range_atr.csv](../analysis/behavior/output/vol_daily_range_atr.csv)). The 1-minute RMS return is **28.9 bps for SOXL and 30.6 bps for SOXS**, and variance scales almost exactly with horizon (h-minute variance divided by h times 1-minute variance is 0.96–1.09 from 5 to 60 minutes), so 1-minute and 60-minute volatility both annualize to about 91–92% ([vol_realized_by_horizon.csv](../analysis/behavior/output/vol_realized_by_horizon.csv)). Leverage scales the semiconductor complex's movement roughly threefold and adds nothing else, as the comparator scorecard shows.

Within the session, movement arrives at predictable times. SOXL's first five minutes are **3.8–4.0× as volatile** as its quietest midday five minutes (157.7 vs 39.4 bps RMS in 2024–26) ([vol_ushape_5min.csv](../analysis/behavior/output/vol_ushape_5min.csv); [chart](../analysis/behavior/output/ushape_30min_primary.png)), the first 30 minutes cover a median 57% of the day's range, and the first hour covers 71% ([open_opening_range.csv](../analysis/behavior/output/open_opening_range.csv)). The day's high or low is set in the first hour on 84–87% of days, but a random walk with the same U-shaped volatility does so 80–83% of the time, so the genuine excess is only 4–6 points ([day_types.csv](../analysis/behavior/output/day_types.csv)). Volume is front-loaded in a way the unlevered funds' is not: the first 30 minutes carry 19.7% of SOXL's RTH minute volume and the last 30 minutes 12.8% (SOXS 8.3%), while SOXX and QQQ put 18.0% and 14.0% into the final half hour ([vol_ushape_30min.csv](../analysis/behavior/output/vol_ushape_30min.csv)). Daily range clusters strongly: its lag-1 autocorrelation rose from 0.31 to 0.53 between the windows, and the median range after a top-quintile day is 10.4% against 5.9% after a bottom-quintile day, which makes yesterday's range a usable forecast of today's movement budget but not of its direction ([vol_clustering_range_acf.csv](../analysis/behavior/output/vol_clustering_range_acf.csv)). Scheduled macro catalysts widened ranges far more in 2022–24, the inflation-and-FOMC regime (detected FOMC days ran at **1.38×** the trailing median range, p < 0.001), than in 2024–26 (1.15×, p = 0.15), when unscheduled tariff and chip headlines produced the widest days. Those event dates were detected from market reactions because official calendars were unreachable from the analysis session, a method that biases their ranges upward ([vol_catalyst_days.csv](../analysis/behavior/output/vol_catalyst_days.csv)).

A large share of the movement happens outside regular hours, and unlike their unlevered peers the funds trade it. SOXL's median absolute opening gap was **2.81%** in 2024–26 and exceeded 5% on 29% of days ([open_gap_distribution.csv](../analysis/behavior/output/open_gap_distribution.csv)). SOXL printed in a median 330 of 330 pre-market minutes, its pre-market range was a median 4.3% of the prior close (62% of the RTH range), and pre-market's share of its volume climbed from **2.8% in 2022Q1 to 13.8% in 2026Q3** ([ext_hours_summary.csv](../analysis/behavior/output/ext_hours_summary.csv); [stability_quarterly_metrics.csv](../analysis/behavior/output/stability_quarterly_metrics.csv)). Pre-market spreads sit near first-half-hour levels, about 8–9 cents for SOXL and 1.4–1.8 cents for SOXS, whereas SOXX and SMH quote 4–8× their midday spread before the open ([quoted_spread_by_bucket.csv](../analysis/microstructure/output/quoted_spread_by_bucket.csv)). The regular session takes out the pre-market high on 61.5% of days and the low on 60.9%, mostly within 15 minutes of the open, and 72–83% of those first breaks trade back through the level within another 15 minutes ([ext_hours_pm_level_breaks.csv](../analysis/behavior/output/ext_hours_pm_level_breaks.csv)). After-hours is thin, at 2.0–2.4% of volume on the median day ([ext_hours_summary.csv](../analysis/behavior/output/ext_hours_summary.csv)). A plausible contributor to the rising pre-market share is Korean retail, which held $5.93B of SOXL on Sept 21, 2026 and trades during Asian daytime ([Seoul Economic Daily](https://en.sedaily.com/finance/2026/09/24/leveraged-etf-executives-flock-to-korea-as-local-buying)). The 20:00–04:00 session runs on alternative trading systems (Blue Ocean suspended SOXL from about Aug 31 to Sep 10, 2026) and falls outside the account's 04:00–20:00 bars; the exchange overnight sessions targeted for December 2026 will widen that uncovered window ([The Investor](https://www.theinvestor.co.kr/article/10860560); [Jones Day](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2026/09/nyse-and-nasdaq-move-to-23hour-trading-day-overnight-session-is-an-evolution-but-not-yet-a-revolution)).

Tails arrive as price jumps inside continuous trading, not as trading pauses. In 2024–26 SOXL made at least one 1-minute move above 1% on 66% of sessions (4.6 a day on average) and above 2% on **12.9% of sessions**, double the 2022–24 rate, while SOXX and QQQ almost never do; the 99.9th percentile of its absolute 1-minute return is 197 bps ([tails_frequency.csv](../analysis/behavior/output/tails_frequency.csv)). The extremes are single-headline minutes: **+8.98% for SOXL and −10.71% for SOXS at 13:19 on 2025-04-09**, and rolling 5-minute moves of +18.8% for SOXL (ending 10:13 on 2025-04-07) and −20.7% for SOXS; in 2022–24 half of SOXL's ten largest minutes fell at 14:00–14:38 ET on detected FOMC days ([tails_top10_moves.csv](../analysis/behavior/output/tails_top10_moves.csv)). Across 1,187 sessions neither fund had a run of five or more missing RTH minute bars, neither had any gap of two minutes or more in 2024–2026, and the LULD-pause quote condition appeared in none of 439.6 million sampled NBBO messages ([tails_rth_minute_bar_gaps_ge5min.csv](../analysis/behavior/output/tails_rth_minute_bar_gaps_ge5min.csv); [rth_trade_gaps_2024_2026.csv](../analysis/microstructure/output/rth_trade_gaps_2024_2026.csv); [luld_quote_flags.csv](../analysis/microstructure/output/luld_quote_flags.csv)). The band mechanics explain the absence: leveraged ETPs sit in LULD Tier 2 with their percentage band multiplied by leverage, 30% for these funds above $3, so a pause requires something like a 10% index move within minutes ([SEC Release 34-101036](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/other/2024/34-101036.pdf); [Cboe LULD FAQ](https://www.cboe.com/document/tech-spec/document/technical-specifications/cboe-limit-updown-faq)). The discontinuity that does recur is the closing print. SOXL's official close deviated from its last continuous trade by more than 100 bps twice, +102 bps on 2022-09-30 and −195 bps on 2025-04-07, the latter followed by a +13.8% opening gap ([tails_closing_dislocations_gt100bps.csv](../analysis/behavior/output/tails_closing_dislocations_gt100bps.csv)).

## Crossing costs about 2 bps, but price level sets the microstructure

Both funds are among the most heavily traded instruments in their peer set. Over the three months to 2026-09-25 SOXL averaged **$8.73bn and 1.11 million trades a day**, nearly SOXX and SMH combined and about six times its 2022 dollar volume, and SOXS averaged $2.82bn and 0.46 million trades ([activity_windows.csv](../analysis/microstructure/output/activity_windows.csv); [activity_yearly.csv](../analysis/microstructure/output/activity_yearly.csv)). In the current price regime (seven sampled days after SOXS's 2026-07-15 reverse split), the time-weighted RTH quoted spread is **5.06 cents (3.66 bps) for SOXL and 1.18 cents (3.16 bps) for SOXS**, and the dollar-weighted effective spread is 1.89 and 2.32 bps, so a marketable order pays roughly 1 bp per side ([quoted_spread_by_bucket.csv](../analysis/microstructure/output/quoted_spread_by_bucket.csv); [effective_spread.csv](../analysis/microstructure/output/effective_spread.csv)). What matters for scalping is the cost relative to the movement it buys. Quoted spread divided by the standard deviation of 1-minute returns is **0.116 for SOXL and 0.103 for SOXS**, in line with NVDA (0.098), TQQQ (0.106) and SPY (0.095) and well below SOXX (0.191), SMH (0.207) and SQQQ (0.231); at 5 minutes the ratio falls to about 0.05 ([spread_to_vol.csv](../analysis/microstructure/output/spread_to_vol.csv)). These estimates rest on 439.6 million NBBO messages and 66.1 million trades in systematically sampled windows across 46 days and nine tickers, which reproduce full-session spreads to a median error of 0.2% ([window_validation_fullday.csv](../analysis/microstructure/output/window_validation_fullday.csv)).

For SOXL, time of day is the dominant cost variable. Its quoted spread falls almost monotonically from **11.6 cents (8.48 bps) in 09:30–09:35 to 2.1 cents (1.52 bps) in 15:55–16:00**, and its effective spread from 5.08 bps in the first five minutes to 0.91 bps in 15:30–16:00 ([quoted_spread_by_bucket.csv](../analysis/microstructure/output/quoted_spread_by_bucket.csv); [effective_spread.csv](../analysis/microstructure/output/effective_spread.csv); [chart](../analysis/microstructure/output/spread_by_time_of_day.png)). Because volatility falls faster than the spread into midday and recovers into the close, SOXL's spread-to-volatility ratio is lowest at the edges (0.101 at 09:30, 0.078 at 15:30) and two to two-and-a-half times higher at 13:30 (0.197). Midday is therefore the most expensive time to trade relative to what the price does, and the last half hour the cheapest ([spread_to_vol.csv](../analysis/microstructure/output/spread_to_vol.csv)). SOXS sits at the one-cent floor 85–99% of the time after 10:30, so its cost varies with time of day only in the first half hour ([quoted_spread_by_bucket.csv](../analysis/microstructure/output/quoted_spread_by_bucket.csv)).

The deeper finding is that the tick regime, and with it most of the microstructure, belongs to the price level rather than to the fund. SOXL spent 90–100% of RTH time at a one-cent spread on every normal sampled day from 2022 through 2025, at closes of $10.60–$55.36; as its price rose in 2026 the one-tick share fell from about 75% at $57 (2026-03-25) to 11% at $98 (2026-04-21) and 1.6% at $164 (2026-05-15) ([sample_day_summary.csv](../analysis/microstructure/output/sample_day_summary.csv); [chart](../analysis/microstructure/output/spread_regimes_by_day.png)). SOXS's relative tick (one cent divided by price) swung from **45–55 bps** in January–February 2026, at a price near $2, to 2.9 bps after the 1-for-20 reverse split of 2026-03-05, back up to 21 bps by June, and to 2.2 bps after the 1-for-10 split of 2026-07-15 ([relative_tick_monthly.csv](../analysis/microstructure/output/relative_tick_monthly.csv)). In the tick-constrained state the book is deep and slow, fills at the touch depend on queue position, the cost in bps is simply one cent over the price, and effective spreads run near 80% of quoted. In the fluid state SOXL's book looks like NVDA's: thin, fast and flickering (51% of NBBO price states last under a millisecond), but cheap to cross thanks to price improvement, with **58% of trades at the midpoint or inside the spread** and an effective-to-quoted ratio of 0.61 against 0.78 for SOXS ([quote_dynamics_by_bucket.csv](../analysis/microstructure/output/quote_dynamics_by_bucket.csv); [effective_spread.csv](../analysis/microstructure/output/effective_spread.csv)). SOXL's displayed bid covers only 0.14 seconds of traded volume, against 0.68 seconds for SOXS ([tick_constraint_summary.csv](../analysis/microstructure/output/tick_constraint_summary.csv)). No sub-penny or half-penny quote appeared in any sampled NBBO message; the SEC's half-penny increment for stocks whose average quoted spread is 1.5 cents or less, a threshold SOXS's current 1.18-cent average falls under, has been deferred to November 2027 ([tick_constraint_summary.csv](../analysis/microstructure/output/tick_constraint_summary.csv); [SEC fact sheet](https://www.sec.gov/files/34-101070-fact-sheet.pdf); [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/06/15/2026-11997/order-granting-temporary-exemptive-relief-pursuant-to-section-36a1-of-the-securities-exchange-act-of)).

Short-horizon adverse selection is measurable but small. Sixty seconds after a trade the midquote has moved in the trade's direction by more than the effective half-spread for both funds (realized spread −0.67 bps for SOXL and −0.47 bps for SOXS; price impact 2.56 and 2.79 bps), and for SOXL the impact sits almost entirely in lit-exchange trades (4.32 bps at 60 seconds) rather than off-exchange prints (0.37 bps) ([effective_spread.csv](../analysis/microstructure/output/effective_spread.csv)). SOXL's 300-second impact rises with size from 2.0 bps at 100 shares to 4.1 bps at 1,000–4,999 shares and 7.0 bps at 5,000–9,999, although the flow is overwhelmingly small: the median SOXL trade is 20 shares and 79% of trades are odd lots, whose quotes the NBBO does not show ([effective_spread_by_size.csv](../analysis/microstructure/output/effective_spread_by_size.csv); [trade_size_oddlot.csv](../analysis/microstructure/output/trade_size_oddlot.csv)). Stress plays out differently in each regime. At sub-$13 prices during the April 2025 tariff crash and rally, SOXL's spread stayed pinned at one cent (8–11 bps) while 1-minute volatility ran 2–5× normal; at $180–200 in June 2026 the fluid spread itself widened to **24–26 cents (11.6–13.7 bps, p90 near 20 bps)**, 4–5× the normal median, with displayed dollar depth at or above normal ([sample_day_summary.csv](../analysis/microstructure/output/sample_day_summary.csv); [stress_vs_normal.csv](../analysis/microstructure/output/stress_vs_normal.csv)). Neither fund has much closing-auction liquidity of its own: the closing cross is a median 1.77% of SOXL's daily volume ($106.5M) and 0.12% of SOXS's ($2.2M), against 10.4% for NVDA ([auctions_summary.csv](../analysis/microstructure/output/auctions_summary.csv)). The microstructure figures carry caveats: the current regime rests on seven sampled days, the stress days were chosen on outcomes, and realized spreads and impacts are the least stable numbers in the set.

## At scalping horizons the path is a random walk scaled threefold

Every serial-dependence test run on SOXL's minute data lands on or near the random-walk value. Deseasonalized lag-1 autocorrelations (returns standardized by day and time of day) are −0.006 at 1 minute and −0.019 at 5 minutes in 2024–26; detrended fluctuation analysis gives an exponent of 0.515 against 0.522 for the same returns shuffled; and runs of same-sign minutes average 1.98 against 2.00 for shuffled data ([dir_autocorr_by_horizon.csv](../analysis/behavior/output/dir_autocorr_by_horizon.csv); [dir_dfa_hurst.csv](../analysis/behavior/output/dir_dfa_hurst.csv); [dir_run_lengths.csv](../analysis/behavior/output/dir_run_lengths.csv)). After a 1-minute move beyond 2σ, with σ estimated from the same half-hour over the prior 20 sessions so there is no look-ahead, the price continues in the same direction **48.5–50.0%** of the time and drifts +0.5 to +2.1 bps over the next 1–30 minutes, trivial next to a 29 bps 1-minute standard deviation ([dir_continuation_after_large_moves.csv](../analysis/behavior/output/dir_continuation_after_large_moves.csv)). The one statistically detectable deviation is mild mean reversion at 15–60 minutes in 2024–26: Lo–MacKinlay variance ratios of **0.974 at 15 minutes and 0.945 at 60 minutes**, both outside a sign-randomized null band ([dir_variance_ratios.csv](../analysis/behavior/output/dir_variance_ratios.csv); [dir_vr_signrandom_null.csv](../analysis/behavior/output/dir_vr_signrandom_null.csv)). It deserves little weight. The result depends on the estimator (the raw non-overlapping VR(60) is 0.98 with a 95% interval of 0.88–1.07), SOXL's VR(60) was 1.002 in 2022–24, and the effect is not a leveraged-ETF property: QQQ (0.905), TQQQ (0.904) and SOXX (0.944) show it in the same window, and the slightly negative 5-minute autocorrelation holds for all eight tickers studied, levered or not ([scorecard_primary.csv](../analysis/behavior/output/scorecard_primary.csv); [chart](../analysis/behavior/output/variance_ratios.png)).

SOXS looks far more mean-reverting, with a VR(60) of 0.818 and a 1-minute autocorrelation of −0.066 in 2024–26, but that is price discreteness in its printed prices rather than behavior of the underlying index. On the 424 sessions when one cent was at least 10 bps of SOXS's price, its 1-minute autocorrelation was **−0.105 and VR(60) 0.741**; on sessions with a 2–5 bp tick they were −0.002 and 0.995, indistinguishable from SOXL ([dir_tick_size_stratified.csv](../analysis/behavior/output/dir_tick_size_stratified.csv)). Across 19 quarters SOXS's median tick size correlates −0.855 with its autocorrelation and −0.871 with VR(15); its worst quarter was 2026Q1, just before the 1-for-20 reverse split, and after the July 2026 split its statistics converged to SOXL's ([stability_tick_size_vs_microstructure.csv](../analysis/behavior/output/stability_tick_size_vs_microstructure.csv); [stability_quarterly_metrics.csv](../analysis/behavior/output/stability_quarterly_metrics.csv)). Because all of these returns come from last-trade prices, bid–ask bounce is not fully removed for either fund; midquote versions were not computed.

The opening's familiar structures are volatility facts rather than directional ones. Same-day gap fills depend on gap size measured in ATR almost identically for all eight tickers, about 90% below 0.1 ATR, 43–53% at 0.25–0.5 ATR and 8–15% above one ATR, so there is no SOXL-specific gap tendency ([open_gap_fill_rates.csv](../analysis/behavior/output/open_gap_fill_rates.csv); [scorecard_primary.csv](../analysis/behavior/output/scorecard_primary.csv)). The 30-minute opening range is broken on 96–98% of days, a median 38 minutes after the open, but the close ends beyond the first break level on only **49.7% of days in 2024–26** (54.0% in 2022–24), with median favourable and adverse excursions of 2.20% and 2.22% ([open_opening_range.csv](../analysis/behavior/output/open_opening_range.csv)). Trend days (a body of at least 70% of the range, closing near the extreme) make up 15–19% of sessions and range days about 30%, close to a random-walk benchmark, slightly trend-heavy in 2022–24 and slightly range-heavy in 2024–26 ([day_types.csv](../analysis/behavior/output/day_types.csv)). The one opening-specific directional effect is narrow. In 2024–26, 1-minute moves beyond 2σ between 09:31 and 10:00 were followed by **+8.4 bps in the next minute (t 3.8; 55.2% continuation; n = 789)**, against +2.9 bps (t 1.6) in 2022–24 ([dir_continuation_after_large_moves.csv](../analysis/behavior/output/dir_continuation_after_large_moves.csv)). That is roughly the size of a full SOXL round trip at that hour once commissions and fees are added to the ~3 bps effective spread, and it rests on one window and has never been traded in the backtests, as discussed below.

VWAP is neither a magnet nor a trend line. SOXL's typical distance from session VWAP after 10:00 is 119–132 bps, or 0.13–0.15 ATR, the same as every comparator in ATR units; it crosses VWAP 6.4–6.9 times a day through a small hysteresis band against 6.5–7.1 for a random walk; and after closing beyond VWAP ± 1σ it returns to VWAP within 30 minutes on **25% of occasions against 29–30% under the random benchmark** ([vwap_behaviour.csv](../analysis/behavior/output/vwap_behaviour.csv)). Revisit rates below random are what a daily directional component (news or drift) produces, not reversion.

The close holds the most consequential directional effect, and it runs against the intuition built on older data. Gao, Han, Li and Zhou's intraday momentum, in which the first half hour predicts the last half hour in S&P 500 ETF data ([SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2440866)), is absent in SOXL, SOXS and SOXX (slope −0.019, t −0.8 for SOXL in 2024–26) ([close_intraday_momentum_ghlz.csv](../analysis/behavior/output/close_intraday_momentum_ghlz.csv)). Instead SOXL's last 30 minutes lean against the day's 09:30–15:30 move, with a **slope of −0.043 (t −2.4) in 2024–26** and the same sign on only 43% of days; the slope was negative in 17 of 19 quarters, though significant only in 2024–26 (−0.006, t −0.5 in 2022–24), and SOXX shows it too, so it originates in the semiconductor complex rather than in the funds ([stability_persistence_summary.csv](../analysis/behavior/output/stability_persistence_summary.csv)). Inside that half hour the path trends, with a deseasonalized VR(15) of 1.12 (z 3.0) against 0.91–0.96 at midday, so the final 30 minutes tend to move persistently and, more often than not, against the day ([dir_variance_ratios_by_time_of_day.csv](../analysis/behavior/output/dir_variance_ratios_by_time_of_day.csv)). The last ten minutes are 1.3–1.4× as volatile per minute as the preceding fifty, and the closing auction then prints a median 6.2 bps (SOXL) and 11.1 bps (SOXS) away from the last continuous trade, again against the day's direction (for SOXL the jump's slope on the day's return has t −6.9 in 2024–26) ([close_last10min_and_auction.csv](../analysis/behavior/output/close_last10min_and_auction.csv); [close_late_day_regressions.csv](../analysis/behavior/output/close_late_day_regressions.csv)). Put together with the cost profile, the session divides into distinct regimes.

## Leverage drift and the SOXL–SOXS mirror are the precise structure

SOXL and SOXS seek +300% and −300% of the daily return of the ICE Semiconductor Index (now branded NYSE Semiconductor Index), which they have tracked since Aug 25, 2021; it holds the 30 largest US-listed semiconductor companies, with the top five capped at 8%, the rest at 4% and ADRs at 10% in aggregate, and ICE has published it every second since January 2022 ([Direxion](https://www.direxion.com/product/daily-semiconductor-bull-bear-3x-etfs); [ICE notice](https://www.ice.com/publicdocs/equity_indices/notices/ICEBIO_ICESEMI_Methodology_Updates_20220128.pdf); [TradingView SOXX description](https://www.tradingview.com/symbols/NASDAQ-SOXX/)). SOXX tracks the same index, and in late September 2026 its heaviest weights were NVDA 9.4%, MU 8.9%, AMD 8.2% and AVGO 7.5% ([TipRanks](https://www.tipranks.com/etf/soxx/holdings)). These fund and index facts come from search-engine extracts, because the primary pages were blocked from the research session. Measured against SOXX, SOXL's 5-minute beta was **+3.00 and SOXS's −3.02** in 2024–26, with correlations of 0.992 and −0.970; against NVDA alone SOXL's 1-minute correlation was only 0.695, so the funds track the capped basket, not its largest member ([link_betas_correlations.csv](../analysis/behavior/output/link_betas_correlations.csv)).

The beta is not a constant 3. Each fund's exposure is set at the prior close and held until the end-of-day rebalance ([Direxion](https://www.direxion.com/education/understanding-leveraged-exchange-traded-funds)), so its effective leverage to the next index increment is L(1+r)/(1+L·r), where r is the index return since the prior close. The measured intraday betas follow this almost exactly. Binned by SOXX's return since the prior close, SOXL's 5-minute beta is 3.41 when SOXX is down about 5% (theory 3.36), 2.97 near zero and **2.63 when SOXX is up about 5%** (theory 2.74), while SOXS's runs from −2.41 on −5% days to **−4.24 on +5% days** (theory −3.71) ([link_leverage_drift_beta_bins.csv](../analysis/behavior/output/link_leverage_drift_beta_bins.csv); [chart](../analysis/behavior/output/leverage_drift_beta.png)). An interaction regression recovers the drift coefficient at −5.84 for SOXL against a first-order theory of −6, and at −13.56 for SOXS against −12; SOXS's overshoot on strong up days is unexplained ([link_leverage_drift_regression.csv](../analysis/behavior/output/link_leverage_drift_regression.csv)). By 15:30 the funds' returns since the prior close track 3× SOXX's with slopes of 2.975 and −2.994 and median deviations of 11 and 16 bps ([link_since_close_tracking_1530.csv](../analysis/behavior/output/link_since_close_tracking_1530.csv)). Two consequences follow for intraday modeling. Fair value has to be anchored to the prior close, not to the open or VWAP, because a constant 3× applied from any intraday reference misprices by an amount that grows with the day's move. And the fund that is losing on the day is the more sensitive one: on a +5% semis day a further 1% index move is worth about 2.6–2.7% in SOXL but 3.7–4.2% in SOXS. The toolkit carries this as features, and over 2022–2026 implied leverage at 15:30 has a 5th–95th percentile range of 2.79–3.27 for SOXL and −3.54 to −2.58 for SOXS ([feature_leverage_tracking_stats.csv](../analysis/backtests/output/feature_leverage_tracking_stats.csv)).

Nothing leads the funds at a trading-relevant resolution. On ten sessions of 1-second data (2026-09-14 to 09-25), a Hayashi–Yoshida estimator that handles asynchronous prints places the cross-correlation peak at **zero seconds for SOXL against SOXX, SMH, QQQ and NVDA on every day**, with lag-zero correlations of 0.72 against SOXX and 0.38 against NVDA ([link_leadlag_1sec_summary.csv](../analysis/behavior/output/link_leadlag_1sec_summary.csv); [chart](../analysis/behavior/output/leadlag_1sec_hy.png)). A naive forward-filled 1-second grid manufactures an NVDA lead (a lead–lag ratio of 12.7) because SOXX and SMH trade in only 46–47% of RTH seconds, against 83% for SOXL and 74% for SOXS ([link_second_bar_coverage.csv](../analysis/behavior/output/link_second_bar_coverage.csv)). At 1 minute, drivers lead SOXL with correlations of just 0.01–0.02 ([link_leadlag_1min.csv](../analysis/behavior/output/link_leadlag_1min.csv)). The backtest battery's lead–lag probe, which trades SOXL at the next open after a 2σ NVDA or SOXX minute, earned −0.5 and −0.1 bps gross out of sample; filled at the signal bar's own open, which is impossible in practice, the same signal would have earned +38.7 and +44.1 bps, a measure of how completely the information is priced within the minute ([battery_timing_diagnostics.csv](../analysis/backtests/output/battery_timing_diagnostics.csv)). The 1-second test covers only ten recent sessions at SOXL prices of $100–150.

The pair mirror each other closely. SOXL and SOXS 1-minute returns correlate at −0.956 (2022–24) and −0.917 (2024–26), the two move the same way in only 7–10% of minutes, and the intraday change in their combined return since 09:35 has a median of about 5 bps ([link_soxl_soxs_mirror.csv](../analysis/behavior/output/link_soxl_soxs_mirror.csv)). The weaker 2024–26 correlation is consistent with SOXS's sub-$10 price spells, when one-cent ticks add noise to its prints. The large divergences are carry-overs from dislocated closing prints rather than intraday breakdowns: episodes of three or more minutes beyond 100 bps rose from 4 to 30 between the windows, and the largest intraday one (232 bps on 2025-04-08) followed SOXL's −195 bps closing dislocation the day before ([link_soxl_soxs_divergence_episodes.csv](../analysis/behavior/output/link_soxl_soxs_divergence_episodes.csv)). The symmetry ends at movement. SOXS's sensitivity rises on up days, its prints are noisier whenever its price is low, it trades in fewer seconds, and it has almost no closing activity: its last half hour carries 7.4% of RTH minute volume and its closing auction 0.12% of daily volume, against 13.2% and 1.77% for SOXL ([volume_profile_minute.csv](../analysis/microstructure/output/volume_profile_minute.csv); [auctions_summary.csv](../analysis/microstructure/output/auctions_summary.csv)).

The end-of-day consequence of leverage drift is a rebalancing trade in the index's direction. Restoring 3× exposure after an index move r requires trading L(L−1)·AUM·r of exposure, which is 6·AUM·r for SOXL and 12·AUM·r for SOXS, both with the sign of r ([Cheng & Madhavan](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1539120)). At reported AUMs of roughly $12–24B for SOXL and $1.1–1.7B for SOXS ([24/7 Wall St](https://247wallst.com/investing/2025/12/15/soxls-13-6-billion-fund-faces-rebalancing-drag-as-memory-cycle-enters-critical-phase/); [YCharts](https://ycharts.com/companies/SOXL/total_assets_under_management); [TradingView](https://www.tradingview.com/symbols/AMEX-SOXS/)), that is about **$0.8–1.7B of semiconductor exposure per 1% index day** and $4–8B on a 5% day, implemented at the end of the day largely through swaps ([Direxion FAQ](https://www.direxion.com/frequently-asked-questions)), in a year when market-wide leveraged-ETF rebalancing reportedly reached a record $50B a day ([Benzinga](https://www.benzinga.com/etfs/broad-u-s-equity-etfs/26/07/60264868/leveraged-etf-rebalancing-hits-50-billion-as-traders-warn-of-a-powerful-force-now-driving-us-market-volatility)). The literature is split on whether such flows move prices. Late-day momentum tied to hedging demand appears across more than 60 futures markets ([Baltussen et al., JFE 2021](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0304405X21001598)), but creations and redemptions can offset most of the rebalancing demand ([Ivanov & Lenkey](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/feds/are-concerns-about-leveraged-etfs-overblown.htm)), and perfectly predictable leveraged-ETF flows attract liquidity provision that shortens their price effect ([Barbon et al.](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3925725)). SOXL's prices side with the skeptics. On the 73 days in 2024–26 when SOXX had moved 2–3% by 15:30, SOXL's last 30 minutes went the same way as its day move only **32.9%** of the time, exactly as often as SOXX's own, and the share stayed below 50% in every size bin; late-day moves grow with the index move (a mean absolute 86 bps on sub-1% days, 157 bps on 3%+ days) but not in the rebalancing direction, whereas QQQ-driven TQQQ continued on 56% of its few 3%+ days ([close_letf_rebalancing_by_index_move.csv](../analysis/behavior/output/close_letf_rebalancing_by_index_move.csv)).

Bars exist for sixteen years, but the usable minute history is shorter. SOXL missed 11.24% of RTH minutes in 2019, 6.65% in 2020 and 1.46% in 2021, then 0.000–0.004% from 2022, and the funds' benchmark changed from the PHLX SOX to the ICE index on 2021-08-25, so 2022 onward (1,187 sessions) is the natural backtest window and 2019–2021 serves only as a robustness check ([dq_coverage.csv](../analysis/backtests/output/dq_coverage.csv); [Direxion](https://www.direxion.com/product/daily-semiconductor-bull-bear-3x-etfs)). The more binding limit is regime history: SOXL's fluid, multi-tick microstructure has existed only since about April 2026 and SOXS's current price level only since the July 2026 split, so execution-sensitive results rest on months, not years, of representative data. Quality is high: all 11.09 million cached bars (2019–2026) are free of duplicate timestamps, off-grid bars, inconsistent highs and lows and non-positive volumes, a raw re-fetch of 12 ticker-months matched the cache exactly, and split factors change only on split dates ([dq_coverage.csv](../analysis/backtests/output/dq_coverage.csv); [dq_raw_duplicate_sample.csv](../analysis/backtests/output/dq_raw_duplicate_sample.csv); [dq_splits.csv](../analysis/backtests/output/dq_splits.csv)).

The traps lie in conventions. Bars are stamped at their start in UTC milliseconds and zero-trade minutes are simply absent. Odd lots update volume but not OHLC (in SOXL's 10:15 bar on 2026-09-25, 5,859 of 7,613 prints were odd lots), extended-hours bars are built from Form-T prints, and bar VWAP falls outside the bar's own range in 6.76% of extended-hours bars ([dq_bar_rebuild.csv](../analysis/backtests/output/dq_bar_rebuild.csv); [dq_coverage.csv](../analysis/backtests/output/dq_coverage.csv)). The closing cross (1,038,341 SOXL shares at $151.45 at 16:00:01.020 on 2026-09-25) sits in the daily bar and in no minute bar, and the official close differs from the last minute close on 86–92% of days by a median 7–10 bps, so any signal that references "the close" has to say which one ([dq_auction_prints.csv](../analysis/backtests/output/dq_auction_prints.csv); [dq_daily_vs_minute.csv](../analysis/backtests/output/dq_daily_vs_minute.csv); [data_auction_print_check.csv](../analysis/behavior/output/data_auction_print_check.csv)). Ten SOXS reverse splits push its split-adjusted 2020 prices to $5–8 million with sub-share volumes, so volume and per-share costs need unadjusted bars; fractional-share volume appears in every ticker from 2026-02-23; and SOXX, the natural index proxy, missed 3.6–7.1% of RTH minutes in 2022–2023, which makes it look like a laggard at 1 minute ([dq_outlier_examples.csv](../analysis/backtests/output/dq_outlier_examples.csv); [data_quality_report.md](../analysis/backtests/output/data_quality_report.md)).

The gap that matters most for tracking is fair value. The account has no ICE Semiconductor Index, no indicative NAV for either fund and no holdings or flows, and its only semiconductor index, I:SOX from 2023-02-15, is the PHLX index with 12/10/8% caps on its top three names rather than the ICE index's 8% caps ([endpoint_inventory.csv](../analysis/backtests/output/endpoint_inventory.csv); [Nasdaq](https://indexes.nasdaqomx.com/docs/202404%20SOX%20Latest%20Changes.pdf)). The toolkit therefore proxies the index with SOXX, an explicit assumption. A licensed 1-second ICE feed would close the gap, and Direxion's daily holdings and premium/discount pages could be scraped forward, though their history and schema were not verified ([Direxion premium/discount](https://www.direxion.com/premium-discount)). Options and futures history cannot support studies of dealer gamma or futures lead before late 2024, and WebSocket streaming and flat-file bulk delivery are documented for the account but were not exercised ([Massive WebSocket docs](https://massive.com/docs/websocket/stocks/aggregates-per-minute); [Massive flat files](https://massive.com/docs/flat-files/stocks/overview)).

### The soxlab toolkit makes the pipeline reproducible, price-aware and look-ahead-tested

The repository's `soxlab` package downloads 1,488 monthly parquet files (8 tickers, adjusted and unadjusted, 2019-01 to 2026-09, about 884 MB) in about five minutes with incremental updates, builds 09:30–15:59 panels that fill forward only causally, and computes 97 look-ahead-safe feature columns plus 3 future-return label columns ([feature_dictionary.csv](../analysis/backtests/output/feature_dictionary.csv); [soxlab README](../soxlab/README.md)). The features span multi-horizon returns and realized volatility, session VWAP with σ-bands, distance and cross counts, 5/15/30-minute opening ranges, pre-market and prior-day levels, gaps in ATR units, relative volume by time of day, EMA states, 1-minute returns and lags for SOXX, NVDA, QQQ and SMH, the leverage-drift set (index return since the prior close, implied and realized leverage, tracking gap), the SOXL–SOXS divergence, and calendar flags for half-days, month-ends, monthly option expiry, quad witching, splits and ex-dividend dates. Quote-based features (spread, depth, imbalance), signed order flow and a macro and earnings event calendar are not yet among them. The cost model converts the microstructure study's half-spread table (cents by ticker, year and half-hour, from 5–14 sampled days per year) into bps at the unadjusted trade price, and adds an assumed $0.0035-per-share commission plus SEC and FINRA sell-side fees ([cost_model_halfspread_detail.csv](../analysis/microstructure/output/cost_model_halfspread_detail.csv); [battery_run_info.json](../analysis/backtests/output/battery_run_info.json)). That price awareness matters. Average round-trip cost on the battery's trades ran **4–10 bps for SOXL and 14–32 bps for SOXS** across 2022–2026, driven almost entirely by unadjusted price (SOXS's mean close was $12.17 in 2025 and it touched $1.59 in February 2026), so a constant-bps assumption would badly misstate SOXS results ([battery_yearly.csv](../analysis/backtests/output/battery_yearly.csv); [activity_yearly.csv](../analysis/microstructure/output/activity_yearly.csv); [price_levels.csv](../analysis/microstructure/output/price_levels.csv)). The harness fills at the next bar's open, resolves stop-first when a bar touches both levels, fills gapped stops at the open and flattens by 15:55, and adds walk-forward runs, a random-side null and lag and shuffle diagnostics. Thirty-two tests pass, including truncation-invariance checks that the features pass and the future-return labels are confirmed to fail, and a daily tracker writes 42 fields per fund per day since 2019 (3,888 rows) with a live REST mode ([daily_tracker_SOXL_SOXS.csv](../analysis/backtests/output/daily_tracker_SOXL_SOXS.csv); [tests](../tests/)).

The same signal on SOXS grossed +20.5 bps out of sample and still lost 3.8 bps net, because its round trip cost 24 bps ([battery_summary.csv](../analysis/backtests/output/battery_summary.csv)).

Everything else is sub-cost, unstable or regime-dependent. The 1-minute EMA crossover is the clearest real but uneconomic effect: +2.7 and +2.0 bps gross a trade over roughly 8,000–11,000 trades per period (t 2.75 in sample), at the 99th and 97th percentile of the random null and positive in six of eight calendar years, yet below a 6.0–8.5 bps average round trip, and still −1.9 bps net at zero commission ([battery_summary.csv](../analysis/backtests/output/battery_summary.csv); [battery_yearly.csv](../analysis/backtests/output/battery_yearly.csv)). Reversal and momentum after large moves flip sign between periods, and fading gaps lost 116 bps a trade gross in 2022 alone. The large-move probes also start trading only at 10:00 and use a 3σ trigger scaled by the prior hour's volatility, so **the battery never tested the opening-half-hour continuation** the price study found ([strategies.py](../soxlab/strategies.py)). The most statistically striking result is a change of behavior rather than an edge. Last-30-minute momentum earned +30.6 bps gross a trade in 2020 and +15.7 in 2021, then turned negative every year from 2023 (−29.8 in 2025), for an OOS gross of −25.7 bps (t −4.34), the trading counterpart of the late-day reversal measured in prices ([battery_yearly.csv](../analysis/backtests/output/battery_yearly.csv)). Across the 48 OOS tests only 7 are net positive, none has a q-value below 0.30, and 30 are significantly negative, mostly because of costs ([battery_multiple_testing.csv](../analysis/backtests/output/battery_multiple_testing.csv)).

The harness is deliberately conservative on timing but coarse on execution. It trades on last-trade minute bars that exclude odd lots and the closing cross, charges an average half-spread rather than the prevailing quote, and assumes a fixed $25,000 notional with no queue position, fill probability, partial fills, market impact or short-borrow cost; and because it flattens at 15:55, it cannot evaluate passive execution inside SOXL's five-tick spread, the closing auction, or anything that depends on where in the bar a stop was touched ([backtest.py](../soxlab/backtest.py); [data_quality_report.md](../analysis/backtests/output/data_quality_report.md)). The trades, quotes and second bars needed for a quote-conditioned fill simulator are already on the account; the harness does not yet use them.

## Conclusion

The measurements move the question from prediction to structure and execution. The directional effects that survive testing are one to three bps a trade against a SOXL round trip of four to ten, and the minute path offers nothing larger to find, so the variables that could change the arithmetic are the ones the current harness abstracts away: where inside SOXL's multi-tick spread an order fills, which half hour it trades in, and how it prices the mechanical relationships that the data measures precisely, namely leverage drift anchored to the prior close, the SOXL–SOXS mirror and the closing-print dislocations that carry into the next session. SOXL is the better-behaved expression of any given signal whenever SOXS trades at a low price, before borrow costs on the short side are counted. The next increment of evidence therefore comes from a quote-conditioned fill model built on data the account already holds, and from the untested opening-half-hour continuation, rather than from more parameter search on minute bars.

The second implication is that neither the microstructure nor the late-day character of these funds is a fixed property, so their history has to be read conditionally. Price level, pushed around by rallies and reverse splits, decides whether SOXS's prints look mean-reverting and whether SOXL's book is one tick wide and deep or five ticks wide and thin. SOXL crossed that boundary only in spring 2026; the half-penny tick now deferred to November 2027 would redraw it for SOXS, and the exchange overnight sessions planned for December 2026 will change what extended hours mean for both funds. The close has already flipped once, from momentum in 2020–21 to reversal since 2023, even as leveraged-ETF rebalancing reached record size, which fits the view that perfectly predictable flows now attract more anticipatory liquidity than they consume. A tracker for these funds should therefore treat unadjusted price, relative tick and the late-day slope as monitored state variables. The out-of-sample record the toolkit's incremental updates accumulate from here will settle more than any further mining of the 2022–2026 sample.
