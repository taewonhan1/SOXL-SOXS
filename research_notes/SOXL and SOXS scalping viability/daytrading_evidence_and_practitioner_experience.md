## 1. Quantified academic and regulatory evidence on the outcomes of day traders and short-term retail traders

### Takeaway
Every large-sample study and regulator dataset found, across Taiwan, Brazil, India, the EU, the US and Korea and from 1999 to 2026, shows the same result. Most retail day traders and short-horizon leveraged traders lose money: typically 70–97%, and loss rates are higher among the most active and the most persistent traders. Traders who are reliably profitable after costs are about 1% or fewer of the population. Zero commissions did not change this. Costs moved into spreads and execution quality, and attention-driven trading predicts negative returns. Leveraged and inverse products made the losses larger, most visibly in Korea's 2026 leveraged-ETF losses.

### Cited Findings

#### Taiwan (Barber, Lee, Liu, Odean and co-authors). The most complete data: all trades on the Taiwan Stock Exchange, 1992–2006
- [R] "The Cross-Section of Speculator Skill: Evidence from Day Trading" (Journal of Financial Markets, 2014). Less than 1% of the day-trader population can predictably and reliably earn positive abnormal returns net of fees. — [ScienceDirect abstract](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1386418113000190); [working-paper PDF](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/Day%20Trading%20Skill%20110523.pdf)
- [R, via secondary summary] Secondary write-ups of the Barber et al. Taiwan work give these figures: roughly 450,000 individual day traders per year, of whom only about 4,000 were predictably profitable in the following year; an average loss of 23.9 bps per day net of fees; aggregate day-trader performance negative in 14 of 15 years. These are summary-level figures and were not checked against the full text, because the PDF host was blocked. — [currentmarketvaluation.com summary](https://www.currentmarketvaluation.com/posts/the-data-on-day-trading.php); [Barber et al. working paper](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/Day%20Traders/Day%20Trading%20and%20Learning%20110217.pdf)
- [R] "Learning, Fast or Slow" (Barber, Lee, Liu, Odean, Zhang; Review of Asset Pricing Studies 10(1), 2020; earlier titled "Do Day Traders Rationally Learn About Their Ability?"):
  - Nearly three-quarters of day-trading volume (74%) comes from traders with a history of losses.
  - In an average month, only 9% of day traders with 400 or more days of experience have positive lifetime intraday net returns.
  - An estimated 97% of day traders are likely to lose money in future day trading.
  - Unprofitable traders are more likely to quit, but too slowly for rational "trading to learn" models to explain continued speculation.
  - Sources: [Oxford Academic](https://academic.oup.com/raps/article/10/1/61/5545095?guestAccessKey=16e65b4b-f232-4ebd-bb9b-5ea5bb2febae); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2535636)
- [R] In Taiwan market data for 1995–1999, individual investors' aggregate trading losses exceeded 2% of Taiwan's annual GDP (Barber, Lee, Liu & Odean, "Who Gains from Trade?" / "Just How Much Do Individual Investors Lose by Trading?"). — [working-paper PDF](http://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/taiwan%20performance/who%20gains%20from%20trade%20040407.pdf)

#### Brazil (Chague, De-Losso & Giovannetti; CVM data)
- [R, CVM data] "Day Trading for a Living?" (SSRN working paper, first circulated 2019), using CVM records:
  - Sample: 19,646 individuals who began day trading mini-Ibovespa futures in 2013–2015.
  - Of those who persisted more than 300 days, 97% lost money.
  - Only 1.1% earned more than the Brazilian minimum wage, and only 0.5% earned more than a bank teller's starting salary.
  - The top earner averaged US$310 per day with a standard deviation of US$2,560.
  - No evidence of learning.
  - Sources: [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3423101); [Tradicted summary](https://www.tradicted.com/research/chagu-day-2020/); [QuantPedia](https://quantpedia.com/retail-day-trading-is-an-uphill-battle/)
- [R, dissent] Porcher (arXiv 2019, "DAY TRADE: do outro lado das estatísticas") rebuts the study:
  - The data cover only two derivatives (mini-index and mini-dollar), not each trader's full activity.
  - The evidence therefore does not prove that day trading as an occupation is unsustainable, or that traders cannot improve.
  - He cites 19,696 starters, of whom 18,138 (92.1%) quit.
  - This is a methodological critique; it offers no counter-data showing profitability.
  - Source: [arXiv 1912.04274](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.04274)
- [IND/PRESS] A newer private study by Grana Capital covered 33,995 Brazilian individuals who bought and sold assets on the same day, Aug 2024–Jul 2025:
  - 71.87% lost money; the mini-index contract (WIN) was the most-used instrument.
  - A search summary adds that 0.18% made profits above R$100k in 12 months, while 1.02% lost more than R$100k. That figure is attributed to Brazilian press coverage, and the underlying report was not seen.
  - Sources: [Monitor Mercantil](https://monitormercantil.com.br/day-trade-causa-perdas-para-7187-dos-investidores-pessoas-fisicas/); [TradingView/Cointelegraph BR](https://br.tradingview.com/news/cointelegraph:9e0172bfdbc81:0/); [BB Investalk (72%)](https://investalk.bb.com.br/noticias/mercado/day-trade-gera-perdas-para-72-dos-investidores-pessoas-fisicas-aponta-estudo)
- [REG/PRESS; source unclear] Brazilian material from CVM and FGV also cites a cohort of 14,748 mini-index starters in 2013–2015. Of these, 92.3% gave up; of the 1,131 who persisted more than a year, 83% had losses. It is not clear which study these figures come from. — [FGV EESP](https://eesp.fgv.br/noticia/viver-de-especulacao-diaria-e-quase-impossivel-mas-tem-cada-vez-mais-brasileiros-fazendo); [CVM Caderno 15 "Day Trade"](https://www.gov.br/investidor/pt-br/educacional/publicacoes-educacionais/cadernos/caderno-cvm-15-day_trade.pdf/@@display-file/file)

#### United States: classic studies
- [R] Jordan & Diltz, "The Profitability of Day Traders" (Financial Analysts Journal 59(6), 2003):
  - Sample: 324 day traders, Feb 1998 – Oct 1999.
  - About twice as many lost money as made money.
  - Only about 20% were more than marginally profitable.
  - Profitability tracked moves in the Nasdaq Composite, i.e. a bull market rather than skill.
  - Sources: [Taylor & Francis](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.2469/faj.v59.n6.2578); [CFA Institute](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/financial-analysts-journal/2003/the-profitability-of-day-traders)
- [R] Barber & Odean, "Trading Is Hazardous to Your Wealth" (Journal of Finance 55(2), April 2000). Among 66,465 households at a large discount broker in 1991–1996, the households that traded most earned 11.4% a year, against 17.9% for the market; the authors attribute this to overconfidence. — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/0022-1082.00226); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=219228)
- [REG] NASAA Day Trading Project Group report (Aug 9, 1999):
  - An independent analysis of sampled accounts at All-Tech Investment Group found that 70% of customers lost money, and only 11.5% showed evidence that they might trade profitably.
  - The consultant, Ronald L. Johnson, wrote that "70% of public traders will not only lose, but will almost certainly lose everything they invest."
  - The sample was small and came from one firm.
  - Sources: [NASAA](https://www.nasaa.org/8219/state-securities-regulators-highlight-problems-with-day-trading/); [CNN Money, Aug 9 1999](https://money.cnn.com/1999/08/09/markets/daytrade/); [report PDF](https://www.nasaa.org/wp-content/uploads/2011/08/NASAA_Day_Trading_Report.pdf)

#### Zero-commission era: US retail evidence
- [R] Barber, Huang, Odean & Schwarz, "Attention-Induced Trading and Returns: Evidence from Robinhood Users" (Journal of Finance 77(6), 2022):
  - The stocks Robinhood users bought most on a given day went on to average −4.7% abnormal returns over 20 days.
  - Five-day abnormal returns were −3%, and −6% for more extreme herding.
  - Robinhood outages disproportionately reduced trading in high-attention stocks.
  - Sources: [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/jofi.13183); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3715077)
- [R] Schwarz, Barber, Huang, Jorion & Odean, "The 'Actual Retail Price' of Equity Trades" (SSRN Sept 2022; Journal of Finance 80(5), 2025):
  - Method: 85,417 simultaneous identical market orders placed through six accounts at five brokers, Dec 2021 – Jun 2022.
  - Mean account-level round-trip cost ranged from 0.07% to 0.46%, excluding commissions.
  - The dispersion comes from wholesalers giving different prices to different brokers for the same trades; differences in payment for order flow (PFOF) do not explain it.
  - Sources: [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/jofi.13467); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4189239); [UCI press release](https://merage.uci.edu/press-releases/2022/10/Uncovering-the-Hidden-Retail-Prices-of-Zero-Commission-Stock-Trades.html)

  - Aggregate retail options losses of about US$2.1bn, Nov 2019 – Jun 2021, mostly from trading costs rather than bad directional calls.
  - Retail traders favour cheap weekly options, which carry an average bid-ask spread of about 12.6%.
  - Sources: [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/jofi.13285); [Ideas for India summary](https://www.ideasforindia.in/topics/money-finance/retail-losses-in-indias-derivatives-market-a-re-examination-of-the-evidence)

### Inferences
- Across datasets from 1999 to 2026, 70–97% of retail day traders lose money. Loss rates rise with trading frequency (SEBI: 80% for traders with more than 500 trades a year; Barber & Odean: the most active quintile earned 11.4% against the market's 17.9%). They also rise with persistence (Brazil: 97% among traders who lasted more than 300 days). A SOXL/SOXS scalper is, by construction, in the highest-frequency bucket.
- Costs make up a large share of retail losses: SEBI found costs equal to a further 57% of losses, and the options studies trace most losses to spreads and fees. This matters even more for scalping, where the gross edge per trade is small.
- The "<1% reliably profitable" result (Taiwan) and the "1.1% earn more than minimum wage" result (Brazil) suggest the realistic question is not whether some scalpers profit — a few do — but whether anyone can identify ex ante that they belong to the top 1%.
- Dissent exists (Porcher on Brazil, Cboe and Bogousslavsky–Muravyev on options). It is about measurement, and none of it produces evidence that most retail day traders profit.
- Leveraged and inverse products add path-dependence and crowding costs to the usual day-trader problems, as shown by Korea's 2026 losses and the Zhao preying mechanism. These are the conditions SOXL/SOXS traders operate in.

### Gaps
- No academic study was found of the realized P&L of US retail day traders in leveraged ETFs (SOXL/SOXS, TQQQ and similar). Brokers do not publish this.
- Full-text verification of several figures was blocked: the Barber et al. 23.9 bps/day and 450,000/4,000 figures, and the exact source of the Brazilian 14,748-cohort figures. Treat them as summary-level.
- The content of the Ideas for India critique of SEBI's figures and the details of Bogousslavsky & Muravyev were not retrieved.
- No 2024–2026 US regulator (SEC/FINRA) data on day-trader loss rates was found. The US has no equivalent of SEBI's disclosure studies or the EU CFD loss-rate disclosures.

## 2. Scalping economics: do intraday edges survive costs in the HFT, market-maker and PFOF era?

### Takeaway
Retail market orders are mostly filled by a handful of wholesalers. Execution quality varies across brokers by amounts (0.07–0.46% round-trip) that are large relative to a scalper's target, and retail limit orders suffer adverse selection. Academic tests of thousands of intraday technical rules find none profitable after data-snooping controls and realistic costs. The documented intraday effects are real but low-frequency: market intraday momentum works once a day, in the last half-hour, and is linked to option-dealer and leveraged-ETF hedging flows. It is not a tick-scalping edge. The widely publicized profitable opening-range-breakout (ORB) and intraday-momentum papers come from authors with commercial ties. Independent replication shows the QQQ ORB edge falls to about zero at roughly 2 cents per share of slippage and is concentrated in 2022.

  - These figures come from search summaries. Which of the two sources below gives each figure was not verified.

- [PRESS] Citadel Securities pays about $2.6bn a year in payment for order flow, mostly for options orders. The claim is from the article's headline. — [The TRADE](https://www.thetradenews.com/citadel-securities-forks-out-2-6-billion-annually-for-payment-for-order-flow-and-most-of-it's-on-options/)
- [R] Mean round-trip execution cost ranges from 0.07% to 0.46% across brokers for identical simultaneous market orders, excluding commissions. For a scalper targeting moves of a few tenths of a percent, this broker choice alone can decide profitability. — [Schwarz et al., Journal of Finance 2025](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/jofi.13467)
- [R] Linnainmaa, "Do Limit Orders Alter Inferences about Investor Performance and Behavior?" (Journal of Finance 65(4), 2010):
  - Data: all individual investors in Finland.
  - Much of their poor post-trade performance comes from using limit orders, which are adversely selected: they get "picked off" when informed traders move the price.
  - Implication: a scalper who "earns the spread" with passive limit orders gets filled disproportionately when the price is about to move against them.
  - Source: [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2010.01576.x)

#### Academic tests of intraday and technical rules net of costs
- [R] Marshall, Cahan & Cahan, "Does intraday technical analysis in the U.S. equity market have value?" (Journal of Empirical Finance 15(2), 2008):
  - Tested 7,846 popular intraday technical rules on the US equity market.
  - Rule families: filter, moving-average, support/resistance, channel breakout and on-balance volume.
  - Two bootstrap methods were used; none of the rules is profitable after data-snooping bias is accounted for.
  - Sources: [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0927539807000588); [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/article/eeeempfin/v_3a15_3ay_3a2008_3ai_3a2_3ap_3a199-210.htm)
- [R] Bajgrowicz & Scaillet, "Technical Trading Revisited" (Journal of Financial Economics 106(3), 2012):
  - Data: daily Dow Jones Industrial Average prices, 1897–2011, analysed with false-discovery-rate controls.
  - Investors could never have picked the future best rules in advance.
  - Even in-sample, "the performance is completely offset by the introduction of low transaction costs."
  - Source: [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0304405X1200116X)
- [R] Studies of intraday FX conclude that technical rules generally do not generate profits intraday, and transaction costs erode what remains. — [academia.edu: "Do technical trading rules generate profits? Conclusions from the intra-day foreign exchange market"](https://www.academia.edu/17344532/Do_technical_trading_rules_generate_profits_Conclusions_from_the_intra_day_foreign_exchange_market)
- [R] Gao, Han, Li & Zhou, "Market Intraday Momentum" (Journal of Financial Economics, 2018):
  - Using SPY data for 1993–2013, the first half-hour return predicts the last half-hour return.
  - The effect is stronger on volatile days, high-volume days, recession days and macro-news days, and also appears in ten other heavily traded ETFs.
  - The authors attribute it to day traders and informed traders.
  - It is a one-trade-per-day timing signal, not a scalping edge.
  - Sources: [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0304405X18301351); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2440866)
- [R] Baltussen, Da, Lammers & Martens, "Hedging Demand and Market Intraday Momentum" (Journal of Financial Economics, 2021):
  - Intraday momentum appears in more than 60 futures across asset classes, 1974–2020: the return before the last 30 minutes predicts the last 30 minutes, then partially reverses over the following days.
  - The authors link it to gamma hedging by option dealers and leveraged ETFs.
  - They cite Cheng & Madhavan's estimate that leveraged-ETF rebalancing made up 16.8% of market-on-close volume on a day with a 1% market move, and 50.2% on a 5% day (as of Feb 2009).
  - Sources: [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0304405X21001598); [author PDF](https://www3.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf)

#### The ORB and intraday-momentum papers most cited by retail day traders, and replications
- [R/IND] Zarattini & Aziz, "Can Day Trading Really Be Profitable? Evidence of Sustainable Long-term Profits from Opening Range Breakout (ORB)..." (SSRN 4416622, posted Apr 10, 2023, revised Apr 21, 2025):
  - Strategy: a 5-minute ORB on QQQ, using TQQQ to get leverage.
  - The TQQQ portfolio returned 1,484% over 2016–2023, against 169% for QQQ buy-and-hold.
  - Andrew Aziz sells day-trading education, which is an interested-party flag.
  - Sources: [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4416622); [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Can-Day-Trading-Really-Be-Profitable-Evidence-of-in-Zarattini-Aziz/4d55f526cc56f08662cb8976796cd3b719ef6d2b)
- [R/IND] Zarattini, Aziz & Barbon, "Beat the Market: An Effective Intraday Momentum Strategy for S&P500 ETF (SPY)" (SFI Research Paper 24-97, May 10, 2024):
  - Uses "noise area" boundaries plus dynamic trailing stops.
  - Reports a 1,985% total return from 2007 to early 2024 net of costs: 19.6% a year, Sharpe ratio 1.33.
  - Sources: [SSRN](https://ssrn.com/abstract=4824172); [SFI](https://www.sfi.ch/en/publications/n-24-97-beat-the-market-an-effective-intraday-momentum-strategy-for-s-p500-etf-spy)
- [R/IND] Zarattini, Barbon & Aziz, "A Profitable Day Trading Strategy For The U.S. Equity Market" (SFI Research Paper 24-98):
  - Applies ORB to more than 7,000 US stocks, 2016–2023, focusing on "stocks in play" (unusually high volume).
  - A QuantConnect re-creation reports a Sharpe ratio of about 2.4 with beta near zero, but the Sharpe ranges from 1.5 to 2.7 depending on the ATR parameter.
  - Sources: [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4729284); [QuantConnect](https://www.quantconnect.com/research/18444/opening-range-breakout-for-stocks-in-play/)
- [R, independent replication] GitHub replication of the Zarattini & Aziz QQQ ORB (Jan 2016 – Feb 2023, 5-minute bars, with an NQ-futures 09:25-bar confirmation filter):
  - It reproduced 1,775 trades (paper: 1,795) and a gross Sharpe of 1.06 (paper: 1.12).
  - Gross P&L was $138,639, or $0.070 per share.
  - Net P&L crosses zero at about 2.2¢ per share of slippage.
  - With $0.02 entry slippage and $0.04 on stops, net P&L falls to $4,860.
  - 76% of the filtered P&L came from 2022 alone, and the strategy lost money in 2017, 2020 and early 2023.
  - Its bootstrap Sharpe confidence interval, [0.05, 1.41], overlaps buy-and-hold's, [−0.03, 1.47].
  - Source: [GitHub, giovannibrusco/zarattini-2023-orb-qqq](https://github.com/giovannibrusco/zarattini-2023-orb-qqq)
- [ANEC/blog replication] "The opening-range breakout paper, replicated on five indices: gross reproduced, net zero" (MQL5 traders' blog, Sep 25, 2026). — [MQL5 blog](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/776235)
- [AGG] Replication commentary notes three problems with the original ORB paper, which uses commissions but no spread or slippage:
  - It has no out-of-sample period.
  - The authors run day-trading education businesses.
  - Published ORB results cluster in 2020–2022.
  - Which of the two sources below made each claim was not verified.
  - Sources: [Papers With Backtest](https://paperswithbacktest.com/strategies/orb-trading-strategy); [danfin.net](https://danfin.net/opening-range-breakout-research)

#### Leveraged-ETF-specific intraday evidence
- [R] Zhao (Aug 2026) documents predatory pre-positioning around leveraged-ETF closing rebalances in Korea: a 75% next-close reversal and a 17.6% cost to holders. The predictable end-of-day flow benefits fast arbitrageurs, not intraday retail traders. — [arXiv 2608.03703](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03703)
- [R] Leveraged-ETF rebalancing is a documented driver of last-30-minute momentum. — [Baltussen et al. 2021](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0304405X21001598)

### Inferences
- Retail scalpers in SOXL/SOXS trade against professional liquidity providers. Marketable orders are internalized by wholesalers, and limit orders are exposed to faster traders. The literature suggests retail limit-order scalping systematically faces adverse selection, and marketable-order scalping pays the spread plus broker-dependent execution slippage on every round trip.
- The only robust, documented intraday effect relevant to SOXL is end-of-day momentum tied to hedging and leveraged-ETF rebalancing. It is a single, slow, crowded signal, and it is contested by faster arbitrageurs (Zhao). It does not support high-frequency scalping.
- The QQQ ORB replication's break-even slippage of 2.2¢ per share is a useful yardstick. Whether SOXL/SOXS scalps can clear their own spreads and slippage depends on SOXL's price and spread, which the liquidity/execution researcher should quantify. The replication also shows that apparent edges concentrate in high-volatility regimes such as 2022, so a 2025–26 backtest may simply reflect regime.
- The profitable-looking day-trading papers come mostly from authors with commercial ties (Aziz, Concretum). Independent academic tests (Marshall et al.; Bajgrowicz & Scaillet) find nothing after costs and data snooping.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study was found of intraday or scalping strategies specifically on SOXL/SOXS or other semiconductor leveraged ETFs.
- No SOXL-specific execution-quality data was found: Rule 605/606 statistics, wholesaler share, price improvement. This is deferred to the liquidity/execution researcher.
- Gao et al.'s trading-strategy return figures and Marshall et al.'s cost assumptions were not retrieved.

## 3. Practitioner experience with scalping SOXL/SOXS: strategies, pitfalls and preferred alternatives

### Takeaway
Almost all SOXL/SOXS practitioner evidence found is anecdote, opinion, or unaudited public scripts. Reddit could not be searched, so this section rests on Substack, Stocktwits, TradingView, Seeking Alpha, blogs and news. Setups are generic momentum and trend tools: EMAs, Supertrend, ATR, volume, VWAP pullbacks and opening-range breaks, sometimes confirmed by index futures. The same pitfalls recur in the sources:
- Gaps magnified three times.
- Stops that do not guarantee fills.
- Averaging down in a 3x product.
- Decay in choppy markets, including holding both legs.
- Crowding.

The June–July 2026 chip sell-off shows the pitfalls concretely. Traders looking for alternatives point to SMH/SOXX for exposure, index futures (MNQ/NQ) for capital efficiency, taxes and no daily reset, and options or single names for defined risk and liquidity. No audited track record of profitable SOXL scalping was found.

### Inferences

### Inferences
- SOXL is a day-trader and retail favourite in both flow and volume terms. Its liquidity partly reflects retail two-way churn, so volume spikes coincide with sharp sell-offs and rebounds, which are also when gap and slippage risk is highest.
- Korean retail is a large, pro-cyclical participant: dip-buying in late July 2026, then selling into the September recovery. Much Korean trading happens in US pre-market and regular hours, which fall during the Korean night. The rebalancing-predation mechanism documented for Korean-listed leveraged ETFs suggests SOXL's predictable closing flows are a profit source for fast arbitrageurs, not for retail scalpers.
- Because retail dominates the flow, the counterparties in a SOXL/SOXS scalp are overwhelmingly professional market makers and arbitrageurs. They profit from spreads and predictable flows, consistent with the adverse-selection evidence in section 2.
